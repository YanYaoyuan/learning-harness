\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\smallpara{Interactive agents and learning from assistance.}
ALFWorld aligns textual household interaction with embodied tasks~\citep{shridhar2021alfworld}; our experiments use its text interface. WebShop evaluates grounded shopping decisions in a simulated website~\citep{yao2022webshop}. ReAct combines reasoning and actions through public demonstrations~\citep{yao2023react}. More recent work explicitly studies skill-guided training and skill-free deployment: SkillScaffold estimates policy-relative skill utility and selectively injects skills during agentic RL~\citep{zhong2026skillscaffold}. Our SFT comparison holds each historical pool fixed, compares matched and global selection spaces, and measures the trained student's behavior under a common executor. It neither reproduces SkillScaffold nor tests an RL update rule.

\smallpara{Predictability and data selection.}
SmartAD selects a correct teacher trajectory with low student NLL and additionally changes segment-level supervision weights~\citep{tang2026smartad}. Our action-level selection omits those trajectory and weighting components. Rho-1 uses reference-based selective token training~\citep{lin2024rho}, while LESS uses optimizer-aware gradient features and target demonstrations~\citep{xia2024less}, which supply the scoring construction instantiated in our global gradient baseline. These methods differ in selection unit, available information, and cost; raw action NLL is not a substitute for implementing them. Our complete comparisons separate likelihood-based and gradient-based selection under explicit admissible support, rather than refute student-aligned selection in general.

\smallpara{Selection, updates, and evaluation.}
EvoHeir separates task admission, parameter inheritance, and experience inheritance, emphasizing that a promising selected task need not yield a useful update~\citep{yang2026evoheir}. Our comparison isolates selection from a fixed experience pool rather than choosing which updated model to inherit. More broadly, few-run RL evaluation can be sensitive to the analysis procedure~\citep{agarwal2021precipice}. We therefore expose crossed seed roles and every paired cell, and keep development evidence distinct from the separately frozen reserved-task replication and restrict inference to the reported finite panels.
